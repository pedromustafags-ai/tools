How this report is put together

Each of the eight checks gets 0 to 3 points from your answer: 3 when it matches what customers say they look for, 0 when a stranger would hold it against you. "Not sure" gets 1, because a stranger sees it whether you've checked or not. With no reviews yet, the star rating and the latest review count as zero, and replies are left out. Your score is your points over the most you could get, out of 100.

The first thing a stranger notices is the check with the fewest points. In a tie, it's the one a stranger usually sees first on the listing: reviews and stars, then photos, hours, review dates and replies, then the website and the phone. The points and the order are our reading, not a measurement.

The survey numbers come from U.S. adults; we haven't found a European survey that asks the same questions. Google's guidance applies in every country where Google Business Profile works.
