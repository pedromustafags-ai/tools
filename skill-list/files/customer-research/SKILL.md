# Customer research

The best line for an ad is usually already written by a customer. This skill finds those lines and keeps them where
the other skills can read them.

## Before you start

Read `brand.md` if it exists: what the business sells, where, and to whom. Ask the user only for what's missing: the
service, the town or area, and the names of two or three competitors.

## Where to look, in this order

1. The business's own reviews, on Google and any other site the user names. If you can't open the page, ask the user
   to paste them.
2. Competitors' reviews, the three-star ones first: they say what the customer expected and didn't get.
3. Competitors' ads, if the user can open them in the platform's ad library and paste the text.
4. The questions people ask about the service online: local groups, forums, the questions under search results. Ask
   the user to paste or link what they find.
5. Once a month, `found-you.md` from the how-found-you skill: how new customers found the business, in their words.

## What to save

Write or update `customer-words.md`, quoting exactly and noting where each line came from:

- What they wanted: the result, in their words
- What worried them before hiring
- What they praised after the job
- What annoyed them, with this business or a competitor
- The words they use for the service, which are often different from the trade's words
- The questions they ask before hiring
- How they found the business, from `found-you.md`

Then copy the three to five strongest lines into `brand.md`, under The buyer, on the "Their words" line. That's the
file every writing skill reads first.

## Rules

- Quote, never paraphrase. Keep their spelling and their slang.
- Leave out reviewers' names and anything personal.
- If a section has no real quotes yet, write "nothing found yet". Never fill it with a guess.
- Finish with three lines for the owner: the phrase that shows up most, the worry that shows up most, and the question
  nobody on their website answers.
